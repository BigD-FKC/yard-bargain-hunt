Privacy policy

Yard Bargains for iPhone and iPad · Effective October 19, 2026

HomePrivacy policyTerms of useSupport and contact

In short: the app's maker collects nothing. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. What you save stays on your iPhone or iPad.

What the app's maker collects

Nothing. Yard Bargains has no server of its own. The app's maker, Andrew Lane, cannot see your Home, your location, the sales you save, your lists, your notes, or your photos.

What stays on your device

  • Your Home, the sales you add, drafts waiting in New Sales, your day's lists and routes, and your notes are stored on your device. So are the town the map shows when you look somewhere other than Home, and the distance you choose in Find Sales. The app's maker has no access to any of them.
  • A later version will also keep them in your own private iCloud storage, so they reach your other devices. Apple holds that storage under its own privacy policy, and the app's maker will have no access to it either. This policy will be updated before that happens.
  • Photos of signs and screenshots you bring in are read on your device. The app keeps the sale details it reads from them, not the people's names or phone numbers that a post may contain.
  • On devices with Apple Intelligence, the app uses Apple's on-device model to read posts and signs. That happens on your device; the text is not sent anywhere.
  • Reminders you set about a sale are scheduled by iOS on your device, as local notifications. Nothing about them is sent anywhere, and the notification shows only the kind of sale, its day, and the first line of your note or when it opens, never its address or what's for sale. iOS asks your permission the first time you set one.
  • The app never keeps a link to a note or anything else in iCloud, such as the link Notes makes when a whole note is shared with Share a Link. If one is shared to the app, it is left out, and the app explains how to stop sharing the note. Such a link is never saved in a draft, a sale or a list.

Shared lists

Sharing lists is coming in a later version. When it arrives:

  • If you share a day's list, it goes through iCloud only to the people you invite, by email address or phone number. Each of them needs the app and an iCloud account.
  • They see the list: its sales, their order, notes, Must-hit and Maybe, which stops are visited or skipped, and whether the day has started or ended. They never see anyone's location, and none of these marks carries a time. A sale's web link goes with it, but never a link to a note or anything else in iCloud.
  • You can remove people or stop sharing at any time, and the list then disappears from their devices. Anyone can leave a list.
  • With Apple's Advanced Data Protection turned on by everyone on a list, the list's details are end-to-end encrypted. Otherwise iCloud encrypts them in transit and on Apple's servers.

Your location

  • The app uses your location only while it's open: to show sales near you, find your address when you set Home, note the street where you photographed a sale sign that gives no address (a hint kept with that draft, never used as the sale's address), start a route from where you are, and, when you tap I'm at a Sale, find the address of the sale you're at, which is kept with that sale like any address you add. It does not follow your location in the background, and it never records where you have been.
  • The first time the map opens, the app explains this, then iOS asks for your permission. You can change your answer at any time in the app's Settings or in your device's Settings app.
  • You don't have to share your location. You can type your Home address instead.

What the app sends to other services, and why

To do its work, the app asks public and Apple services for information. These requests carry no name, account, or advertising identifier from the app. Like any internet request, they reach the service from your device's IP address.

  • Apple Maps: where you are or a place you search for, to draw the map, find the address where you are, find businesses that hold sales, place sales at their addresses (or at the place a post names instead, such as a church), and work out driving times between stops.
  • Esri (ArcGIS Hub): when you use Find Sales, the area it is looking at, to find garage-sale data that cities publish in Esri's public catalogue. The app shows each city's own page as a link; it doesn't read the data.
  • City open-data sites: the area you're looking at, to fetch that city's published garage-sale permits, where it publishes them.
  • Websites you open: listing sites such as Craigslist, Kijiji or EstateSales.NET open in your web browser, as they would if you visited them yourself. The app itself never downloads or reads those pages.
  • Navigation apps: when you tap Start, Next, Skip or Directions Again, the next sale's address is handed to the map app you chose in Settings: Apple Maps, Google Maps or Waze. Whole Route hands Apple Maps up to 15 sales at a time. To list only the map apps on your device, the app asks iOS whether Google Maps and Waze are installed; the answer stays on your device.

Each of those services handles requests under its own privacy policy.

Children

The app is not directed to children, and it collects no personal information from anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because your data is on your own device, you control it. You can delete any sale, draft, or list in the app. To erase them all at once, use Erase All Sales and Lists at the bottom of the app's Settings; it keeps your settings, such as Home. Deleting the app removes all its data from that device.

Old sales clear themselves away. Thirty days after a sale's last day, the app deletes it, and a past day's list, with its visited and skipped marks, is deleted 30 days after that day. Nothing about them is kept, so the app holds no history of where you went.
