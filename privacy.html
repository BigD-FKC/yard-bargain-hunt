Privacy policy

Yard Bargains for iPhone and iPad · Effective October 30, 2026

HomePrivacy policyTerms of useSupport and contact

In short: the app's maker collects nothing. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. What you save stays on your iPhone or iPad, and in your own private iCloud if you're signed in to it.

What the app's maker collects

Nothing. Yard Bargains has no server of its own. The app's maker, Andrew Lane, cannot see your Home, your location, the sales you save, your lists, your notes, your photos, or what you buy and sell in Flip It.

What stays on your device

  • Your Home, the sales you add, drafts waiting in New Sales, your day's lists and routes, and your notes are stored on your device. So are the town the map shows when you look somewhere other than Home, the distance you choose in Find Sales, and any place you choose for a day's Start From or End At, such as a hotel, which is kept only until that day has passed. The app's maker has no access to any of them.
  • Spots you mark, places to come back to such as a sale sign you passed, are kept only on the device you marked them on: never in iCloud, on your other devices, or on a shared list. A spot keeps its place, the street it's near and the day you marked it, never the time, and it is deleted by itself 14 days after that day, or when you remove it, add a sale from it, or use Erase All Sales and Lists.
  • If you're signed in to iCloud, the sales you add, your drafts, your day's lists with your order and how far the day has got, and your notes are also kept in your own private iCloud storage, so they reach your other devices signed in to the same account. Apple holds that storage under its own privacy policy, and the app's maker has no access to it. Your settings, such as Home, your reminders and your spots stay on each device. Erase All Sales and Lists deletes your sales and lists on all your devices and in iCloud.
  • Flip It, for things you buy to resell, keeps each find you add: what it is, its barcode if you scan one, what you paid, the day you bought it, the kind of sale and the town (such as "Lawrence, KS", never an address or a map location), where it's kept, listed and sold, its prices, fees and shipping, your notes, and up to six photos. Photos are made smaller, and where a camera photo was taken isn't kept. It also keeps the other costs you add, such as booth rent, and your selling places and their fees. These are stored on your device and, if you're signed in to iCloud, in your own private iCloud, like your sales; they're never on a shared list or in a copy you send. Your profit goal and usual selling place stay on each device. Finds stay until you delete them: they don't clear away with their sale.
  • Check an Item reads the words and barcode in its photo on your device. If you tap Not Buying, nothing is kept, not even the photo. Get Ready to Sell hands a find's photos and a line about it to the app you choose in the Share menu, such as a selling app or Messages; Yard Bargains never posts a listing itself. Export makes spreadsheet files of your finds and other costs, and they go only where you send them.
  • Photos of signs, and screenshots and posts you bring in, are read on your device: in the app, or by its Share card when you share a post or screenshot to Yard Bargains. The card shows what it read; if you tap Add to List, it hands the sale's details, and the days you chose, to the app on your device, which puts the sale on those lists. The app keeps the sale details it reads, not the words or pictures, and not the people's names or phone numbers that a post may contain. If a post writes its address differently from the address Apple's search found for it, the post's way of writing the address is kept with the sale while it waits in New Sales, so you can compare them, and is removed once the sale goes on a list. Terms a post gives for the day, such as half price on Sunday or a sign-up sheet, are kept as a short note in the app's own words, never the post's, and never with a phone number. The app never opens a shared link to read the page.
  • On devices with Apple Intelligence, the app uses Apple's on-device model to read posts and signs. That happens on your device; the text is not sent anywhere.
  • Reminders you set about a sale are scheduled by iOS on your device, as local notifications. Nothing about them is sent anywhere, and the notification shows only the kind of sale, its day, and the first line of your note or when it opens, never its address or what's for sale. iOS asks your permission the first time you set one.
  • The app never keeps a link to a note or anything else in iCloud, such as the link Notes makes when a whole note is shared with Share a Link. If one is shared to the app, it is left out, and the app explains how to stop sharing the note. Such a link is never saved in a draft, a sale or a list.

Shared lists

You can share a day's list with people you choose:

  • If you share a day's list, it goes through iCloud only to the people you invite, by email address or phone number. Each of them needs the app and an iCloud account. You choose whether each person can make changes or only view it.
  • They see the list: its sales, Must-hit and Maybe, notes, and which sales have been visited or marked cancelled. A shared list runs the day Together unless someone who can change it chooses Each on Their Own. On a Together list they also see its route: the order of its sales, how it was put in order, and which sales were skipped, so everyone has the same next sale. Each person's times, their Start From and End At, and when they start or end their day are never shared, and on a list set to Each on Their Own neither are their order or the sales they skip. They never see anyone's location, and a visited, skipped or cancelled mark carries no time. A sale's web link goes with it, but never a link to a note or anything else in iCloud.
  • You can remove people or stop sharing at any time, and the list then disappears from their devices; you keep it. Anyone can leave a list. Erase All Sales and Lists deletes a list you shared for everyone on it, and leaves the lists others shared with you.
  • If you join a list for a day you had already planned, the app tells you first: your sales for that day go onto the shared list, where everyone on it can see them, or back to New Sales if you can only view it.
  • With Apple's Advanced Data Protection turned on by everyone on a list, the list's details are end-to-end encrypted. Otherwise iCloud encrypts them in transit and on Apple's servers.

Sending a copy

You can send a copy of one sale, or of a day's list, as a single message anyone can read: each sale's kind, address, hours, what's for sale, notes, and a link to it in Apple Maps. You choose how it goes, such as Messages, Mail or AirDrop, and who gets it; the app itself sends nothing anywhere. A copy never includes your Home, your order, your times or anyone's location, and a list someone else shared with you is sent without its notes. Once sent, a copy is the other person's, like any message.

A copy you paste into the app becomes drafts in New Sales, one for each sale; the message itself isn't kept.

Your location

  • The app uses your location only while it's open, or when you ask Siri to mark a spot: to show sales near you, find your address when you set Home, note the street where you photographed a sale sign that gives no address (a hint kept with that draft, never used as the sale's address), start a route from where you are, and, when you tap I'm at a Sale, find the address of the sale you're at, which is kept with that sale like any address you add, and, when you tap Mark This Spot or ask Siri to, mark a spot where you are, which stays on that device and clears away after 14 days. It does not follow your location in the background, and it never records where you have been. The one exception is yours to keep or delete: each find you add to Flip It keeps the day you bought it, the kind of sale and its town.
  • The first time the app needs your location, it explains this, then iOS asks for your permission. You can change your answer at any time in the app's Settings or in your device's Settings app.
  • You don't have to share your location. You can type your Home address instead.

What the app sends to other services, and why

To do its work, the app asks public and Apple services for information. These requests carry no name, account, or advertising identifier from the app. Like any internet request, they reach the service from your device's IP address.

  • Apple Maps: where you are, a place you search for, a spot you marked, the point where you put the pin in Pick on the Map, or the middle of the map when you tap Show This Area, to draw the map, find the address where you are, name the street a spot or a picked sale is near, name the town the map shows or a find was bought in (from the sale's spot, to about a kilometer), find shops such as flea markets and thrift stores, place sales at their addresses (or at the place a post names instead, such as a church), looking around Home or around the town the map shows when you're away, and work out driving times between stops.
  • Esri (ArcGIS Hub): when you use Find Sales, the area it is looking at, to about a kilometer, to find garage-sale data that cities publish in Esri's public catalogue. The app shows each city's own page as a link; it doesn't read the data.
  • City open-data sites: the area you're looking at, to about a kilometer, to fetch that city's published garage-sale permits, where it publishes them.
  • Websites you open: listing sites such as Craigslist, Kijiji or EstateSales.NET open in your web browser, or in the site's own app if you have it, as they would if you visited them yourself. A link that needs a place, such as Craigslist's or Kijiji's, carries the area to about a kilometer, never your exact spot. The app itself never downloads or reads those pages.
  • Price websites: when you tap eBay Sold Prices or Local Asking Prices in Check an Item, its search words (or, for eBay, the barcode) go to eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist or Kijiji in your web browser, or in that site's own app if you have it, as they would if you searched there yourself. Craigslist and Kijiji also get the area to look in, to about a kilometer, and Kijiji the town. Nothing goes to them until you tap, and the app never reads those pages.
  • The App Store: to start your free trial, buy, restore your purchases or redeem a code, the app uses Apple's own In-App Purchase system, which works with your Apple Account. Apple keeps the record of what you bought and when your trial began, under its own privacy policy; the app reads it on your device and keeps no record of its own, and the app's maker receives nothing that identifies you. A test version from TestFlight doesn't use Apple's system for this: it keeps when your trial began and what you chose on your device only, and sends it nowhere.
  • Navigation apps: when you tap Start the Day, Next, Skip or Directions Again, the next sale's address is handed to the map app you chose in Settings: Apple Maps, Google Maps or Waze. Whole Route hands Apple Maps up to 15 sales at a time. To list only the map apps on your device, the app asks iOS whether Google Maps and Waze are installed; the answer stays on your device.

Each of those services handles requests under its own privacy policy.

Children

The app is not directed to children, and it collects no personal information from anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because your data is on your own device, and in your own iCloud, you control it. You can delete any sale, draft, or list in the app. To erase them all at once, use Erase All Sales and Lists at the bottom of the app's Settings; it keeps your settings, such as Home, and if you're signed in to iCloud it erases your sales and lists on all your devices and in iCloud. It doesn't touch Flip It: in Flip It, under Your Records, Erase All Finds erases your finds, their photos and your other costs, and Clean Slate also erases your selling places, on all your devices and in iCloud. Deleting the app removes all its data from that device; what's kept in your iCloud stays there, and comes back if you install the app again, until you erase it.

Old sales clear themselves away. Thirty days after a sale's last day, the app deletes it, and a past day's list, with its visited and skipped marks, is deleted 30 days after that day. Nothing about them is kept, so the app holds no history of where you went, apart from the day, kind of sale and town kept with each find in Flip It.
