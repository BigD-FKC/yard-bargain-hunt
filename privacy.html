Privacy policy

Yard Bargains for iPhone and iPad · Effective October 14, 2026

HomePrivacy policyTerms of useSupport and contact

In short: the app's maker collects nothing. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. What you save stays on your devices and in your own iCloud.

What the app's maker collects

Nothing. Yard Bargains has no server of its own. The app's maker, Andrew Lane, cannot see your Home, your location, the sales you save, your lists, your notes, or your photos.

What stays on your devices and in your iCloud

  • Your Home, the sales you add, drafts waiting in Sales to Check, your day's lists and routes, and your notes are stored on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud storage, so they reach your other devices. Apple holds that storage under its own privacy policy; the app's maker has no access to it.
  • Photos of signs and screenshots you bring in are read on your device. The app keeps the sale details it reads from them, not the people's names or phone numbers that a post may contain.
  • On devices with Apple Intelligence, the app uses Apple's on-device model to read posts and signs. That happens on your device; the text is not sent anywhere.

Shared lists

  • If you share a day's list, it goes through iCloud only to the people you invite, by email address or phone number. Each of them needs the app and an iCloud account.
  • They see the list: its sales, their order, notes, Must-hit and Maybe, and which stops are visited. They never see anyone's location, and visited marks carry no time.
  • You can remove people or stop sharing at any time, and the list then disappears from their devices. Anyone can leave a list.
  • With Apple's Advanced Data Protection turned on by everyone on a list, the list's details are end-to-end encrypted. Otherwise iCloud encrypts them in transit and on Apple's servers.

Your location

  • The app uses your location only while it's open: to show where you are, measure distances, notice when you're away from Home, and plan routes. It does not follow your location in the background.
  • You don't have to share your location. You can type your Home address instead.

What the app sends to other services, and why

To do its work, the app asks public and Apple services for information. These requests carry no name, account, or advertising identifier from the app. Like any internet request, they reach the service from your device's IP address.

  • Apple Maps: where you are or a place you search for, to draw the map, find businesses that hold sales, place sales at their addresses, and work out driving times between stops.
  • Esri (ArcGIS Hub): the area Discover is searching, to find garage-sale data that cities publish.
  • City open-data sites: the area you're looking at, to fetch that city's published garage-sale permits, where it publishes them.
  • Websites you open: listing sites such as Craigslist, Kijiji or EstateSales.NET open in Safari, as they would if you visited them yourself.
  • Navigation apps: when you tap Go, your route's stops are handed to Apple Maps, or to Google Maps or Waze if you choose.

Each of those services handles requests under its own privacy policy.

Children

The app is not directed to children, and it collects no personal information from anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because your data is on your devices and in your own iCloud, you control it. You can delete any sale, draft, or list in the app, and stop sharing any list. Deleting the app removes its data from that device; you can remove the app's iCloud data in Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage.
